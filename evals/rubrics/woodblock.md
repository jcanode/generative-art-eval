# Woodblock print (moku-hanga manner)

The image should read as a traditional Japanese-style colour woodblock print: carved key lines, flat colour blocks, graded wiping.

## Key block line
A dark carved outline defines the forms: confident, tapering, slightly irregular lines. 1 = no outlines or vector-uniform lines. 3 = outlines present but mechanical. 5 = expressive carved key lines that hold the design together.

## Flat colour blocks
A limited set of flat colours printed inside the key lines, with slight misregistration. 1 = continuous tone or halftone. 3 = mostly flat but with too many colours or airbrushed areas. 5 = clean flat blocks with a harmonious limited palette.

## Bokashi gradation
Graded wiping on selected blocks (sky darkening toward the top, water deepening), and no other gradients. 1 = none, or gradients everywhere. 3 = gradients present but digital-looking. 5 = convincing bokashi used where tradition puts it.

## Print surface
Wood grain, baren rubbing texture, soft pigment edges, paper showing through. 1 = sterile digital. 3 = texture present but filter-like. 5 = convincing printed surface.

## Stylised nature and composition
Nature rendered as pattern (waves, clouds, rain), with a strong, often asymmetric composition. 1 = naturalistic or cluttered. 3 = some stylisation. 5 = elegant patterned forms in a striking composition.

## Subject clarity
Subjects are immediately identifiable. 1 = unreadable. 3 = identifiable with effort. 5 = everything reads at a glance.
