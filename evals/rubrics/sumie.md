# Sumi-e ink painting

The image should read as East Asian ink-wash painting: black ink, brush and water on paper.

## Monochrome ink
Black ink in a range of dilutions on warm paper; at most one small accent (for example a red seal). 1 = full colour. 3 = mostly monochrome but with distracting colour or flat greys. 5 = rich range of ink values from wet grey to dense black.

## Brushwork
Marks look like they were made by a bristle brush: tapering, pressure changes, dry-brush breakup ("flying white") where the brush ran out of ink. 1 = vector lines or uniform strokes. 3 = brush-like but mechanical. 5 = confident, varied, living strokes.

## Wash and water
Diluted ink washes with soft bleeding and darker pooled edges where the water dried. 1 = no washes, or flat digital fills. 3 = washes present but uniform. 5 = convincing wet-in-wet behaviour and pooled edges.

## Economy and empty space
Empty paper is part of the design: sky, water and mist are left unpainted; few strokes say a lot. 1 = canvas filled edge to edge. 3 = some breathing room but overworked. 5 = masterful restraint, and the emptiness reads as space.

## Subject clarity
With so few strokes, subjects are still immediately identifiable. 1 = marks don't cohere into subjects. 3 = subjects identifiable with effort. 5 = each subject reads instantly from a handful of strokes.
