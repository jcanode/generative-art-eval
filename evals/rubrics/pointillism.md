# Pointillist painting (in the manner of 1880s divisionism)

The image should read as a divisionist painting: the whole surface is built from small dabs of unmixed colour that blend in the eye.

## Dot construction
The surface is made of distinct small dabs or dots, not strokes, fills or gradients. 1 = smooth or brushy surface. 3 = dots present but mixed with flat areas or long strokes. 5 = everything is dots, of consistent size and deliberate placement.

## Optical colour mixing
Colours are divided: areas are built from dots of different pure hues that mix at a distance, and shadows contain complementary colour. 1 = each area is a single flat colour. 3 = some variation but mostly tints of one hue. 5 = convincing optical mixing with complementary contrast.

## Luminosity and contrast halos
Light and dark forms meet with a halo (lighter beside dark, darker beside light), and the image glows. 1 = dull or muddy. 3 = some luminosity but flat. 5 = radiant light with visible halos at edges.

## Calm structure
Clear, stable composition with still, simplified silhouettes defined by dot density rather than drawn outlines. 1 = chaotic or formless. 3 = readable but loose. 5 = serene, well-ordered forms.

## Subject clarity
Despite the dots, subjects are immediately identifiable. 1 = subjects dissolve into noise. 3 = identifiable with effort. 5 = every subject reads at a glance.
