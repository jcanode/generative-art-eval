# Mid-century screenprint

The image should read as a hand-pulled screenprint in the tradition of 1950s–60s travel and exhibition posters.

## Limited inks
Built from a small number of flat inks (about 3–5) plus paper colour. 1 = continuous photographic colour or many unrelated colours. 3 = mostly flat but with some gradients or too many hues. 5 = clearly a handful of flat inks, and paper shows through as a colour.

## Overprint and layering
Inks interact the way printed layers do: overlaps make a third colour (multiply), and shapes knock out to paper. 1 = no sense of layers. 3 = layered but flat, no overprint colours. 5 = deliberate overprints and knockouts used as part of the design.

## Halftone and screen tone
Tone and shading come from halftone dots or line screens, not smooth gradients. 1 = smooth airbrush shading. 3 = some screen texture but unconvincing or uniform. 5 = halftone and line screens model light, depth, or texture convincingly.

## Print imperfection
Evidence of the physical process: slight misregistration, ink starvation, speckle, uneven density, paper texture. 1 = sterile vector graphic. 3 = texture present but reads as a digital filter. 5 = convincing hand-pulled print character that doesn't obscure the image.

## Graphic composition
Bold silhouettes, clear figure/ground, strong design. 1 = muddled or cluttered, weak silhouettes. 3 = readable but ordinary. 5 = striking poster composition where every shape reads instantly.
