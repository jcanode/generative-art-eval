# Impasto oil painting (in the manner of late-1880s post-impressionism)

The image should read as a thickly painted oil canvas in which every area is built from short, directional, visible brushstrokes.

## Directional brushstrokes
The whole surface is made of distinct strokes whose direction follows form and movement (swirling skies, terrain, contours of objects). 1 = flat fills or random texture. 3 = strokes present but directionless or uniform. 5 = stroke direction describes form and energy everywhere.

## Movement and rhythm
Skies and fields have flowing rhythm: vortices, eddies, sweeping curves. 1 = static. 3 = some flow but mechanical or repetitive. 5 = the whole surface feels alive and rhythmic without becoming noise.

## Colour
Saturated, expressive colour with complementary contrasts (blue against yellow, green against red-orange), and per-stroke colour variation. 1 = dull or photographic colour. 3 = colourful but unrelated or muddy. 5 = vibrant, harmonious complementary colour.

## Contour
Dark, broken outlines separate major forms. 1 = none, and forms dissolve. 3 = present but uniform or too heavy. 5 = expressive, broken contour lines that strengthen the forms.

## Paint body
Visible thickness: ridged strokes, bristle grooves, relief catching the light. 1 = flat digital image. 3 = texture present but reads as a filter. 5 = convincing thick paint.

## Subject clarity
Despite the stroke texture, subjects are immediately identifiable. 1 = subjects lost in the texture. 3 = identifiable with effort. 5 = every subject reads at a glance.
